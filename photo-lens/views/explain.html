lens how it works connecting…

Your photos never leave this Mac.

Lens looks at each photo once, remembers what is in it, and finds it when you ask. There is no account, no upload and no internet in any part of that.

how it works

1
You pick which folders it may look in.
2
It looks at every photo once — where it was taken, when, and what is in the picture.
3
It writes what it learned into a private notebook on your own disk.
4
When you search, it reads the notebook — never the internet.

Each box is a link to the part of this page that proves it. Keyboard: Tab to a box, then Enter.

Details for nerds — the seven things step 2 and 3 actually do
  1. Walk the folders you chose. POST /roots · caches, app bundles and dot-folders are never walked (indexer.SKIP_DIRS)
  2. Read every EXIF tag, byte for byte. metadata._dump_exif → every base/Exif/GPS tag, kept verbatim in raw_exif
  3. Render one small thumbnail per file. thumbs.ensure_thumb → {sha1}-512-v2.webp, written to a temp name and moved into place
  4. Embed that thumbnail with SigLIP2 on the local GPU. embed.Embedder.embed_images · torch mps when available, else cpu · 1,152 float16 numbers per image
  5. Write the facts to one SQLite file, the vectors to one npz. catalog.sqlite + embeddings.npz · vectors checkpointed every 128 images, so a kill never loses the run
  6. Answer each query from a local daemon. one numpy dot product over the stored matrix · ~45 ms warm on this library · no index server, no network hop
  7. Refuse to talk to anything but this machine. the daemon binds 127.0.0.1 only, reflects CORS to loopback origins alone, and refuses a non-loopback Host header

Details for nerds — the actual ranking, in eight lines

A query is first read for structure — dates, place names, camera names, the word "trip" — and whatever words are left over become the residual. The residual is wrapped in a photo of a {} and pushed through the model's text tower, which returns a vector in the same 1,152-dimensional space as the images.

"Cosine similarity" here is nothing more exotic than a dot product: both sides were divided by their own length when they were made (embed._normalize), so the lengths are already 1 and the division in the cosine formula has nothing left to do. The scores are small positive numbers: on this library a real answer for "beach" tops out around 0.121, and the plateau of unrelated frames sits near 0.039.

Because the range depends on the model, no fixed threshold transfers. The cut is a proportion of the best hit instead: half of the top score. Anything above that line is called a match; if fewer than twelve rows clear it, the twelve closest are returned anyway so you can judge rather than be told "nothing".

who is in your photos

Lens looks for faces in your pictures and puts the ones that keep coming back together into a person. It never guesses a name — you type it, or it comes across from a name Apple Photos already had. Nobody outside this Mac is ever asked who anyone is.

Details for nerds — the two local models, the thresholds and why they are what they are

Two models, both local. MTCNN finds the faces — a box and a confidence, and nothing at all about identity. Each box is cropped square and pushed through InceptionResnetV1 with vggface2 weights, which returns a 512-dimensional unit vector: two crops of the same person land close together on that sphere, two different people land apart. That is the whole of "recognition" here — a distance. The detector runs on the CPU whatever else the machine has, because its image pyramid needs an adaptive pool MPS refuses at non-divisible sizes; the embedder does run on MPS, where its input is a fixed 160×160. For a video it is the middle keyframe, already decoded for the thumbnail. The rows land in a faces table and the vectors in cache/faces.npz, checkpointed every 32 photographs.

The grouping is a threshold, and the threshold was measured. Nobody has told lens who these people are, so the groups have to be found: it walks the faces in a fixed order, drops each one into the nearest cluster whose centre it is within 0.65 of, starts a new cluster when it is not, then recomputes the centres and does it again. Two passes, hand-rolled — sklearn's agglomerative clustering wants the whole distance matrix in memory (20,000 faces is a 1.6 GB triangle) and 30 MB of dependency for one function.

The 0.65 was read off this library rather than chosen. At the size it was when the rule was settled — 86 photographs, 67 faces, 2,211 pairs — the median similarity of two faces is 0.13 and the ninetieth percentile 0.49, while one person across five years, two file formats and a pair of sunglasses ran 0.59 to 1.00 (median 0.81 inside a cluster of eleven, checked by eye crop by crop). At 0.60 the largest cluster had swallowed a second man and a face-painted stranger along with the eleven who belong together; at 0.65 they stay apart; 0.70 only sheds a sighting. The asymmetry is the whole argument for erring high: joining two people puts a wrong name on somebody's photographs and you have to notice before you can undo it, while splitting one person in two gives you two cards to merge in one press.

A face seen once or twice is not a person. It is a stranger in the background of a street scene — and a card, with a name field, for somebody you have never met. Under three sightings a face keeps its row and its vector and belongs to nobody, and is promoted the moment a third one turns up.

A person stays the same person. Clustering runs again after every index run and recomputes from scratch, but a name you typed and a merge you made are not in the vectors and cannot be recomputed. They are anchored to something that can: the cluster's centre. A fresh cluster whose centre is within 0.8 of a person lens already knows is that person, and inherits their id, their name and any merge they were part of. Centres barely move — measured drift after two of one person's eleven faces were removed was 0.003, against 0.49 between the two closest different people in the library — so that is a wide margin rather than a lucky one. A merged person keeps their row with merged_into set, and it is their centre that makes the merge outlive the recompute.

A name is never invented. Either you typed it, or it came across from a name your Apple Photos library already holds — and then only from photographs with exactly one detected face and exactly one name, only once a cluster has agreed with itself three times, never over a name you typed, and never when two names tie. A group shot's five names against five faces is a permutation problem, not a fact, so it is left alone. Everything else is unnamed and stays unnamed. Nothing is looked up anywhere: there is no identity service, nothing is uploaded, and the two models were downloaded once from HuggingFace and GitHub when lens was installed. Face crops are cut from the thumbnail lens already has and served, like every other byte on this page, over the loopback-only daemon.

Once a person has a name it becomes a word. photos of Ana then means her face rather than a folder called Ana — the parser matches the people you have named before it tries anything else — and two names narrow rather than widen, because a photograph with both of them in it is what "Ana and Ben" asks for.

None of this is one of the seven steps above. The face pass needs one extra package (see the last section); without it lens indexes and searches exactly as it does now, and the index run says why there are no faces.

what lens can’t do (yet)

Every one of these is a decision somebody made on purpose, not a bug waiting to be fixed.

  • ≈Your Apple Photos library is left alone unless you switch it on. Switched on, lens reads the originals where they already sit — nothing is copied, nothing is written back. Photos iCloud has moved off this Mac are counted and skipped, never downloaded.
  • ≈A video is judged by a handful of frames. Lens takes six stills spread across the clip and blends them into one impression, so it finds what a video is mostly about and can miss something that flashes past in a second.
  • ✕Lens never guesses who somebody is. All it notices is that the same face keeps turning up. A name exists because you typed it, or because Apple Photos already had it and agreed with itself three times over. A face seen once or twice is deliberately nobody.
  • ≈Faces need one extra piece of software installed. Without it everything else works exactly the same, and lens tells you that is why there are no faces.
  • ✕Some folders are never looked in. System folders, applications, developer caches and anything whose name starts with a dot. Photos parked in one of those are invisible to lens.
  • ≈Lens can’t tell nonsense from a real search. A real word picks out a handful of photos; pure gibberish comes back as a “good match” for most of your library. So lens never claims your words were meaningless — it says when the search failed to separate your photos, and drops the good-match line because there is nothing there to draw it through. Try the gibberish chip above and watch it happen.
  • ≈Telling a real photo from a screenshot is a good guess, not a certainty. A camera name or a location settles it. A photo stripped of both can land on the wrong side, and so can an exported graphic that kept them.
  • ≈A photo with no date borrows the date the file was saved. That is when the file was written, not when the picture was taken — which is one reason trips are built only from real camera photos.
  • ≈A place is the nearest named town, not an address. It is worked out offline from a built-in list of place names, which is why “bali” works and a street name never will.
  • ≈A file lens cannot open still counts. It is included in the size of your library and left out of every search.
  • ⏱The very first run is slow, once. It downloads about 3.5 GB for the AI that looks at your photos, then works through your library. After that it only looks at files that have changed.
Details for nerds — the constants, file names and exact rules behind those eleven lines
  • ≈Apple Photos — off by default (apple_photos in the cache's config.json, or the ⚙ panel). The bundle is never walked (indexer.SKIP_SUFFIXES = (".photoslibrary",)): it holds every original and every derivative under Masters/resources, so walking one would index each picture several times over. Switched on, lens reads the library's own database through osxphotos and indexes the originals in place, read-only.
  • ≈Video — .mp4, .mov, .m4v and .webm are indexed (metadata.VIDEO_EXTS); .mkv and .avi are not, because on a real machine those are downloaded films. Six frames sampled evenly across the duration, embedded, and mean-pooled into one vector. Frame-level matching is a later wave.
  • ✕Identity — a face joins a person by distance alone (persons.JOIN = 0.65), and a name only crosses from Apple Photos over single-face photographs whose cluster has agreed three times, never over a name you typed and never when two names tie.
  • ≈The faces package — pip install --no-deps facenet-pytorch. The --no-deps is not a shortcut: its declared pins are two years stale (torch<2.3, numpy<2, Pillow<10.3) and honouring them downgrades all three underneath the rest of lens, after which pillow-heif refuses to load and HEIC photos stop being readable.
  • ✕Skipped directories — Library, Applications, System, node_modules, __pycache__, venv and everything beginning with a dot.
  • ≈Gibberish — measured when this library held 86 photographs, “beach” cleared the bar for 3 of them and “boat” for 4, while asdkjhasd kjhasd cleared it for 84 of 86; the demo above is the same experiment against the library as it stands now. The model finds something for any string, and its scores for gibberish are indistinguishable from a real low-signal query like “food”.
  • ≈Photograph versus graphic — a camera name or a GPS fix is conclusive; failing those, a capture format (JPEG/HEIC/AVIF/DNG…) carrying DateTimeOriginal or DateTimeDigitized. DateTime alone is deliberately not trusted, because editors and screenshot tools write it.
  • ≈Undated files — with no DateTimeOriginal and no DateTime, taken_at falls back to the filesystem mtime.
  • ≈Places — reverse geocoding is offline (reverse_geocoder), so a coordinate resolves to the closest populated place and its GeoNames region.
  • ≈Unreadable files — counted in the library's size and excluded from every search (error IS NULL). That is the gap between the two numbers in the first two cards of the readout above.
  • ⏱First run — roughly 3.5 GB of SigLIP2 weights before a single photo is embedded, then minutes of GPU work. Everything after that is incremental: a file is re-read only when its size or mtime changed.
  • ⏱Every query re-reads the matrix — vectors are loaded from embeddings.npz per query rather than pinned in the process, which the operating system's page cache makes cheap (~45 ms warm here) but is not the same as an in-memory index. The first query of a session also pays for the text tower's first forward pass.
